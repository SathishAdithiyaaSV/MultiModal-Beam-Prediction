# Meeting notes — 8 Oct 2026

Adaptive modality routing for mmWave beam prediction. DeepSense6G 2022,
scenarios 31–34. All numbers on `val`, n = 2,198. DBA = distance-based accuracy
(Δ=5).

---

## 1. Where we were

The modality ablation gave us the setup for everything since:

| configuration | DBA | GFLOPs |
|---|---|---|
| GPS + Camera | **0.8835** | 48.24 |
| full 4-modality | 0.8759 | 94.10 |
| GPS alone | 0.7298 | **0.39** |

Two modalities beat four at half the cost, and **GPS alone gets 83 % of the
accuracy for 0.4 % of the compute** — a 240× cost gap. That gap is the whole
opportunity: if we could tell *which* samples actually need the camera, we would
pay for it only on those.

So: **cheap tier = GPS, expensive tier = GPS + Camera.** Everything below is
about deciding, per sample, which to use.

---

## 2. Oracle routing — is the opportunity real?

**What we did.** No training. For each validation sample we asked: which tier
got it right? Then we routed perfectly, with hindsight. This is a ceiling, not a
method — it tells us whether the idea is worth pursuing at all.

**Result — yes, clearly.**

| | Top-1 | GFLOPs |
|---|---|---|
| always cheap (GPS) | 0.3203 | 0.39 |
| always expensive (GPS + Camera) | 0.4604 | 48.24 |
| **oracle router** | **0.5496** | **11.36** |

Perfect routing beats always-expensive by **+0.089 Top-1 at a quarter of the
compute**, escalating only 23 % of samples.

**But the obvious gate does not work.** Escalating the least-confident samples
using the cheap model's own confidence:

| target | escalation needed | compute saved |
|---|---|---|
| match always-expensive DBA | **87 %** | 13 % |

Escalating ~9 in 10 samples to save ~1 part in 8. Confidence gives **AUC 0.686**
for "the cheap tier already suffices". Also sobering: **45 % of samples are
wrong under both tiers** (routing cannot help them), and **8.9 % are actively
hurt** by escalating.

**We also closed a planned line of work cheaply.** Every beam-ambiguity
difficulty measure scored *below* raw confidence (0.633, 0.593, 0.591 vs 0.686)
— and because they are computed from the ground-truth target, they were the
*ceiling* for that approach. **Difficulty-aware gating is ruled out. We are not
building it.**

**Conclusion.** The headroom is real and measured. The bottleneck is the *gate
signal*, not the idea.

## 3. The three follow-up notebooks

All three are built. One has been run.

### 3a. `learned_adaptive_gate.ipynb` — ✅ **run, full result**

**What it does.** Replaces the confidence threshold with a learned gate: a
**2,113-parameter MLP** over **31 inference-available features** — the cheap
model's uncertainty (entropy, top-1/top-2 margin), the sensor availability mask,
GPS displacement statistics, and image/LiDAR/radar quality statistics. Two
objectives are compared: `classify` (predict the binary "cheap wrong, expensive
right") and `regress_gain` (predict the continuous per-sample DBA gain).

*No beam label, power vector, or correctness signal is ever a gate input.* Those
build the training target only. Fitted on `train` (8,844), evaluated on `val`.

**Result — the learned gate works.** Escalation needed to reach
always-expensive DBA:

| policy | escalated | GFLOPs | compute saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| oracle (ceiling) | 26 % | 12.83 | 73 % |
| **learned gate (`regress_gain`)** | **66 %** | **31.97** | **34 %** |
| learned gate (`classify`) | 84 % | 40.58 | 16 % |
| confidence threshold | 87 % | 42.02 | 13 % |

DBA at fixed escalation budgets:

| policy | @10 % | @25 % | @50 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| oracle | 0.8155 | 0.8819 | 0.9134 |
| **`regress_gain`** | **0.7919** | **0.8372** | **0.8662** |
| confidence | 0.7653 | 0.8199 | 0.8557 |
| `classify` | 0.7552 | 0.7958 | 0.8393 |

Three takeaways:

1. **Beats confidence at every budget** — +0.027 DBA at 10 % escalation,
   +0.017 at 25 %, against a ±0.011 paired standard error. It saves **34 % of
   compute where confidence saves 13 %**, roughly 2.6×.
2. **The regression objective is the right one.** `classify` trails by 0.03–0.04
   throughout and loses even to plain confidence below 50 % escalation — the
   binary label throws away *how much* each sample stands to gain. We are
   dropping that arm.
3. **It transfers.** Leave-one-scenario-out, gate never sees the held-out
   scenario: it recovers **72 % / 63 % / 76 %** of the cheap→expensive gap on
   scenarios 32 / 33 / 34.

### 3b. `modality_robustness.ipynb` — ✅ **run, full result**

**What it does.** Two questions.

*RQ1 — degradation curves and tipping points.* Sweeps each modality from intact
to severely degraded and finds where accuracy collapses: camera blur (σ up to
8 px), camera noise, camera occlusion (up to 60 % of frame area), camera
resolution (down to 1/16), LiDAR point dropout (down to 2 % of returns), GPS
position noise (0–20 m, which spans consumer-GNSS error). The point is to learn
*at what degradation level camera stops being worth its 48 GFLOPs* — which is
exactly the threshold a router should trigger on.

*RQ2 — missing modalities.* Does AMBER's eq. (3) masking mechanism actually hold
up when a sensor drops out, compared with training a model for that subset
directly? And does *natural* missingness (scenario 34 ships radar for only ~40 %
of samples) behave like *synthetic* masking?

**Result — four findings, two of which change how earlier results read.**

**(a) Camera is fragile.** Severity at which GPS + Camera (48.24 GFLOPs) falls
*below* free GPS alone (DBA 0.7298, 0.39 GFLOPs):

| degradation | tipping point | in plain terms |
|---|---|---|
| blur | **σ ≈ 1.3 px** | mild defocus |
| noise | **σ ≈ 0.084** | ~8 % intensity noise, a dim night scene |
| occlusion | **≈ 17.5 % of frame** | a dirt patch or raindrop |
| resolution | **≈ 0.42 downscale** | roughly half linear resolution |

None of these are extreme. A σ=2 blurred frame still looks normal, and at that
point the camera costs 124× more than GPS and performs *worse*. These are
exactly the thresholds a cost-aware router should trigger on.

**(b) The GPS-only cheap tier needs better-than-consumer GNSS.** Consumer GPS is
1–5 m. At 5 m error, GPS alone falls from **0.7298 to 0.4367** — it loses 40 % of
its DBA. Our "GPS alone gets 83 % of full accuracy at 0.4 % of compute" headline
is **true for DeepSense's high-precision positioning and must be quoted with
that qualification.** Conversely, GPS + Camera barely moves (0.8835 → 0.8360 at
*20 m*): **camera is the redundancy modality, not just the accuracy modality** —
a second, independent argument for it that partly offsets the generalisation
trap.

**(c) AMBER's availability masking does not degrade gracefully.** Masking a
modality vs training a model for that subset, DBA:

| modalities | masked | trained | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| gps + radar | 0.3746 | 0.7794 | **−0.4048** |
| gps + lidar | 0.4117 | 0.7770 | **−0.3654** |
| gps + image | 0.8703 | 0.8835 | −0.0132 |
| gps + radar + image | 0.8756 | 0.8789 | −0.0033 |

The pattern is exact: **masking is fine whenever camera survives and
catastrophic whenever it does not.** Eq. (3) handles *redundant* missing
modalities; it does not survive losing the one the model actually learned to
use. Practical consequence: **a cheap tier cannot be built by masking the
expensive model — tiers must be independently trained.** That is what we do, so
our results stand, but it closes off the cheaper alternative.

Dropping one modality from the full model also confirms the ablation by a
different route: lidar −0.0003, radar −0.0012, gps −0.0214, **image −0.4373**.
LiDAR and radar are within noise of doing nothing.

**(d) Natural missingness is confounded.** Samples where radar is genuinely
absent score *higher* (DBA 0.9046, n=453) than where present (0.8684, n=1,745),
because missingness concentrates in scenario 34. It cannot be used as a proxy
for synthetic masking.

**Design note worth raising.** We rejected several degradations as unfaithful
rather than running them for the sake of a plot:
- **Camera brightness and contrast are provable no-ops.** AMBER standardises
  each image by its own mean and std, which is affine-invariant — verified
  numerically to 4e-7. Any lighting experiment here measures nothing.
- **Radar degradation cannot be done faithfully** — the stored tensor is a
  post-FFT magnitude map, and map-domain noise has no physical pre-image. We use
  the dataset's own radar missingness instead.
- **LiDAR range truncation is near-vacuous** — the BEV is already cropped to the
  ±50 m region holding 99 % of points.

### 3c. `modality_quality_signal.ipynb` — ⚠️ **run, but the run is void**

**What it does.** Asks which inference-available signals predict "the cheap tier
suffices", scored by AUC against the oracle label, with confidence's **0.686**
as the benchmark to beat. It then groups features by *what they cost to obtain*:

- **Group A** — cheap-model confidence only.
- **Group B** — A, plus uncertainty statistics, the availability mask, and GPS
  statistics. **Everything obtainable before any expensive sensor is read.**
- **Group C** — B, plus image and LiDAR quality statistics.

**Status — a bug made the central comparison impossible, and it is now fixed.**
Groups B and C returned **bit-identical AUC to 16 decimal places** despite C
having 31 features against B's 18. Cause: the dataset returns zero tensors for
any modality outside the configuration's slug, and our cheap tier is GPS-only —
so all 13 image/LiDAR/radar quality statistics were identically 0.0 for every
sample. Constant features contribute nothing, so C collapsed exactly onto B.
Fixed by loading every modality when collecting features and restricting the
*model* through the availability mask instead (numerically equivalent, Δlogits
= 0.0). A guard now raises rather than letting the comparison go quietly vacuous.
**The notebook needs re-running.**

What survives is the no-sensor-read ranking, on 300 samples: `top1_conf` 0.657,
`margin_15` 0.656, `top2_conf` 0.641, `entropy` 0.610 — consistent with the
oracle study's 0.686 and confirming the confidence family leads. One oddity to
check at full scale: group B (18 features, 0.566) scored *worse* than confidence
alone (0.650) on held-out scenarios, which looks like overfitting on 300 samples.

**This also touches the gate result.** The gate's 31 features included the same
13 constant columns, so it was effectively using 18. Its headline — 34 % compute
saved vs confidence's 13 % — **stands**, because zero-variance features
contribute exactly zero. But the gate has never been given sensor-quality
information, so that number is a floor rather than a ceiling. Worth re-running.

**Why the grouping matters, and this is the part worth discussing.** It depends
on what we think is expensive. Under a **processing-cost** model — which is what
we measure, forward GFLOPs — computing a cheap image sharpness statistic and
*then* deciding whether to run ResNet34 is a genuine saving. Under a
**sensing-cost** model, where powering and reading the sensor is the expense, it
is not: you had to read the camera to compute the statistic. **Group B is the
honest number if sensing dominates; Group C if processing dominates.** We report
both so the claim can be read either way.

## 4. What the results mean

**The routing premise survived contact with the data, but in a modified form.**
Our original plan was a Radar+GPS cheap tier escalating on difficulty. Both
halves died: radar does not pay for itself (+0.050 DBA for 23 GFLOPs, and it
*hurts* alongside camera), and difficulty-aware gating scores below plain
confidence. What replaced them — a GPS cheap tier with a learned regression gate
— works better than either.

**We have a real, quantified efficiency result.** 34 % compute saved at matched
accuracy, against 13 % for the standard confidence baseline, with cross-scenario
transfer. That is a defensible contribution.

**But the strongest finding may not be the routing one.** The ablation says
something more uncomfortable and more interesting: *most modalities in
multimodal beam prediction do not pay for themselves, and the one that pays most
in-domain generalises worst.* Camera configurations average 0.8764 on seen
scenarios and **0.0567** on the unseen scenario 31 — where **GPS alone becomes
the best configuration of all eight**. Since **48 % of the official test split
is scenario 31**, this is not a footnote. It cuts against the field's push
toward adding more modalities.

**Suggested framing:** lead with the modality-economics and generalisation
result; the routing work becomes its constructive answer.

## 6. Honest limitations

- Single training run per configuration; no seeds, no error bars.
- Scenario 31 is n = 50. Generalisation claims rest on the *consistent ordering*
  across all eight configurations, not on individual values.
- Routing is measured on validation only — in-domain by construction. Transfer
  is tested leave-one-scenario-out, but scenario 31 cannot be tested that way,
  as it has no training samples.
- 45 % of samples are wrong under both tiers, which caps any routing gain.
- Scenario 34 supplies radar for only ~40 % of samples, so radar configurations
  are partly GPS-only there — this *understates* radar.
- **The GPS-alone headline assumes high-precision positioning.** At consumer-GNSS
  error (5 m) the cheap tier loses 40 % of its DBA.
- Models trained 10 epochs, not to convergence. Best epochs were 8–10, so the
  ranking is likely stable, but absolute numbers would rise.
